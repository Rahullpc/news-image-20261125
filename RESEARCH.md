## Lead story — the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi oil

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two oil tankers struck naval mines and
caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz early on Wednesday, claiming the ships
used an unauthorised route with their navigation systems switched off. The
fires were in the tankers' engine rooms. The same day, Yemen's Houthis said
they had attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery — the kingdom's largest
— one day after a Houthi strike on a tower in Riyadh injured 11 people. It
was the sharpest escalation of the Iran war's maritime front in weeks:
attacks in the Strait had soared in October to their highest level since the
war began at the end of February, and ran near-daily through November.

What is deliberately left out: no tanker names, flags or crew figures were
published in the statement, and a later claim of a third tanker hit by a
mine came only from IRGC-linked outlets with no independent confirmation,
so neither appears on the poster. No oil price is printed either — the
figures circulating that day directly contradicted each other.

## The six grid stories

### 1. Tariffs — Washington, trade

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs on 14 major trading partners
after an eight-month Section 301 investigation into "excess industrial
capacity", with the highest rates aimed at China. The partners named were
China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, the EU, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea,
Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam. Proposed
maximum rates run 20-40%, while existing trade deals generally cap them at
10-20%. Duties were still subject to public comment and a hearing. The move
came three weeks after midterm losses and on the eve of Thanksgiving.

### 2. Huawei — New York, technology and law

A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei on 10 of 12 counts — bank fraud,
wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets — but
acquitted the company of the most serious charge, racketeering. The case
began in 2018 over Huawei's business in Iran and alleged theft of US
technology. Sentencing was set for 14 September 2027. The trial lasted two
to three months with more than 60 prosecution witnesses.

### 3. Space — Jiuquan to Tiangong

Shenzhou-24 launched from Jiuquan at 08:18 Beijing time and docked with
China's Tiangong space station about three and a half hours later. Aboard
was payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali — the first Pakistani in orbit
and the first foreign astronaut ever to visit Tiangong. The crew was
commanded by Tang Hongbo, on his third Tiangong visit, with Wu Yi. Ali
stays about a week and returns aboard Shenzhou-23.

### 4. Cairo fire — Egypt, disaster

A fuel tanker overturned and burst into flames on Cairo's Ring Road in the
Al-Warraq district of Giza, engulfing more than two dozen vehicles. Egypt's
Health Ministry put the toll at 31 dead and 21 injured, calling the count
preliminary; prosecutors put the number of vehicles caught in the fire at
27. The crash site was above Tunnel 10 on the Ring Road.

### 5. Solomon Islands — Canberra, diplomacy

Australia and Solomon Islands signed a treaty in Canberra making Australia
the Solomons' primary security partner and precluding the establishment of
any foreign military or security base on Solomon Islands soil. It was signed
by Prime Ministers Anthony Albanese and Matthew Wale, and follows the
Solomons' 2022 security pact with China. Claims that the new treaty "ends"
the 2022 pact come only from secondary sources, so the poster states only
the verified core: the new treaty and the no-foreign-base clause.

### 6. Sudan — Blue Nile, conflict

Drone strikes by Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces killed 19
civilians and wounded 20 in Damazin and Roseires, in Blue Nile state,
hitting residential areas, electricity facilities and a dam, according to
the Sudanese rights group Emergency Lawyers. The UN also criticised deadly
drone strikes in Blue Nile.
